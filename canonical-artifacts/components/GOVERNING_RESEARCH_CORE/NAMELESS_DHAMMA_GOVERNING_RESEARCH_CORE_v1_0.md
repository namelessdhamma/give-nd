## Meaning of direct realization

The expressions “direct realization” and “непосредственное постижение” refer to actual realization rather than merely conceptual representation of Nibbāna. They must not be interpreted as requiring a reified subject who realizes, a separate instrument performing realization, or a concept substituted for what is realized. Direct does not mean causeless. Direct does not negate conditioned cultivation of the path. The Russian phrase “путём развития випассаны с опорой на сатипаттхану” designates the conditioned cultivation leading toward realization; it does not introduce an intermediary inside realization itself. Preserve both “непосредственному постижению” and “путём развития”.

## Mandatory interpretive invariants

### Nibbāna is not produced

Conditions conduce to path cultivation and realization. They do not produce Nibbāna, cause Nibbāna to arise, make Nibbāna conditioned, or transform Nibbāna into an outcome manufactured by practice.

### Broad scope of paccaya

Do not reduce paccaya in advance to one meditation technique, one immediate mental configuration, formal meditation alone, cetasikas alone, or immediately preceding conditions alone. Potentially relevant conditions may include sīla, samatha, wise attention, hearing the Dhamma, suitable friendship, bodily conditions, environmental conditions, previous cultivation, meditation instructions, ethical restraint, seclusion, and any other evidenced condition. Relevance must be established through research.

### Scope of the auxiliary branches

The two auxiliary questions are subordinate to the central question. They must not become independent studies of all wholesome or unwholesome dhammas. Every auxiliary investigation remains constrained by direct realization of Nibbāna through vipassanā grounded in Satipaṭṭhāna. The two branches are not assumed to form an exhaustive partition of every possible condition.

### Contextual classification

Do not classify a dhamma as conducive or obstructive merely by its name, traditional reputation, habitual assumption, or a permanent binary label. Classification requires explicit textual evidence, a reconstructed conditional relation, a clearly marked inference, or an explicitly provisional hypothesis. The same phenomenon may have different conditional roles in different contexts. Preserve those contextual differences.

### Positive branch distinctions

Do not collapse arising, development, fulfillment, maintenance, restoration after decline, maturation, or stabilization. For each claim, record the process actually supported by the evidence.

### Obstructive branch distinctions

Do not automatically interpret non-arising, weakening, abandonment, and cessation as four necessary successive stages. Distinguish where evidence permits temporary non-arising, situational suppression, attenuation, abandonment, cessation, eradication, and irreversible future non-arising. Do not collapse suppression, attenuation, abandonment, cessation, and eradication into one category.

### Degree of realization

Do not automatically equate sacchikiriyā with final arahantship. Where sources permit, distinguish first breakthrough, first direct realization, an intermediate noble stage, final destruction of the āsavas, and unspecified realization. When the source does not establish the degree, record UNSPECIFIED. Do not infer finality.
