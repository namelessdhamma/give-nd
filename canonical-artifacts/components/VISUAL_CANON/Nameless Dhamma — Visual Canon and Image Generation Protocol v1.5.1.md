NAMELESS DHAMMA
VISUAL CANON AND IMAGE GENERATION PROTOCOL

Version 1.5.1 · 8 September 2026

Status: canonical project standard — DAE-aware constitutive integration
Primary working language: English
Language policy: no language is prohibited. Significant canonical technical terms must be written in Pāḷi; Romanized Pāḷi is permitted. Pāḷi, Thai, English, and Russian may be used whenever functionally useful for precision, clarification, quotation, philology, practice instruction, or communication.

PURPOSE

This document is the primary operational standard for designing, generating, editing, and auditing every Nameless Dhamma image. Before any generation, the system must open this document, establish one precise Dhamma thesis, classify the composition, complete the project specification, compile the prompt, and audit the result.

The workflow is:
Dhamma basis → thesis → composition classifier → project specification → prompt compiler → generation → audit.

The document separates five functions:
1. Canon — invariant visual ontology, semantic rules, and prohibitions.
2. Composition classifier — selection of the spatial and temporal structure that best matches the Dhamma mechanism.
3. Project specification — complete resolution of the individual artwork before prompt writing.
4. Prompt compiler — conversion of the approved specification into model instructions.
5. Audit — decision to accept, regenerate, or perform one surgical correction.

I. CANON

1. Ontological core

1.1 One conditional field
There are no independent objects anywhere in a Nameless Dhamma image. Figure, body, anatomy, symbol, architecture, background, light, and space are temporary configurations of one continuous conditional field.

A recognizable image is permitted, but it must never appear self-existing. A Buddha figure does not stand inside a separately prepared environment; figure and environment arise from the same field. A skull is not placed on top of a network; it is a temporary concentration of that network.

1.2 One visual substance
Every visible form is constructed from the same material language:
• fine filaments;
• luminous or dark nodes;
• intersections and dependencies;
• translucent connections;
• local concentrations and dispersals;
• dissolving boundaries;
• negative space.

A dense surface may appear only as an optical consequence of many relations. It must not become an independent opaque mass.

1.3 Recursive conditionality
The network must remain intelligible at three scales:
• macro: the whole composition is a system of relations;
• meso: figures and symbols are built from those relations;
• micro: lines and nodes imply still finer networks.

Zooming in should not reveal homogeneous matter. It should continue to reveal conditions, connections, and temporary configurations.

1.4 Arising, alteration, and cessation
Every major form should imply:
• arising;
• temporary stabilization;
• alteration;
• loosening or dispersion;
• disappearance into the shared field.

This expresses anicca. It is not explosion, catastrophe, or decorative destruction. Dissolution should indicate the weakening or ending of supporting conditions, not the destruction of an independent entity.

2. Semantic core

2.1 One artwork, one primary Dhamma thesis
A work may be structurally complex, but it must communicate one principal statement.

The primary thesis may be derived from a mature DAE constitutive analysis, a full PAE conditioned-relation map, a PLM practice reduction, or a justified hybrid. Use the representation that best serves the artwork. A mature DAE result may govern what the represented process is, how it is constituted across time, and which discriminator must remain visible; it does not by itself establish a PAE-owned causal relation. When a mature PLM pointer is used, its prioritized practice hinge should normally govern the primary thesis and attention path; the broader PAE network becomes supporting structure only when it materially improves meaning. Non-exhaustiveness is not a visual defect when omitted constitution or conditions do not change the practical meaning.

Required formulation:
“This image must make visible that…”

Examples:
• pleasant feeling sustains continuation through craving;
• sati is not an observing self but the non-loss of the relevant process;
• personality is a repeated act of appropriation;
• when supporting conditions cease, no owner of cessation can be found.

2.2 Recognizable semantic anchor
Every work requires one clear semantic anchor, such as:
• a figure;
• a fused symbol;
• a wheel;
• a gate;
• a path;
• a sequence of states;
• a distributed system;
• a polar pair.

The anchor must be quickly recognizable, doctrinally precise, and subordinate to the thesis. It is always made from the same field as the background and never possesses fully closed independent boundaries.

2.3 Background as condition
The background is not decoration. It must do at least one of the following:
• reveal conditions of arising;
• reveal consequences;
• continue the structure of the anchor;
• expose hidden relations;
• show the field from which forms arise and into which they cease.

If a background element can be removed without loss of Dhamma meaning, remove it.

2.4 No false subject
When representing sati, consciousness, knowing, observation, or clarity, do not create:
• an eternal witness;
• a soul inside the body;
• an independent center of awareness;
• a subject standing outside conditioned processes.

A figure may represent a function, never a metaphysical entity.

3. Zero zone: absence where a core is expected

Every work must include a zero zone: a visible absence at the place where a stable core, owner, or essence would normally be expected.

Possible realizations:
• a point in the background color inside a luminous node;
• a missing central node;
• an aperture;
• a break in the network;
• an empty region at the convergence of forces;
• a receding opening;
• an unfilled space within a recognizable form.

The zero zone may occupy the geometric, perspectival, or semantic center. The black point within neutral light remains a central brand sign, but it must not be inserted mechanically when another form of absence expresses the thesis more accurately.

4. Background, node-field morphology, and light

4.1 Black background
Black is the primary Nameless Dhamma mode. It represents absence, non-assertion, and the disappearance of configurations. It must not automatically read as literal outer space.

Star-like and nebula-like morphology is permitted when it is the visible behavior of the conditional network itself.

Permitted:
• nodes that resemble stars because they are intersections of relations;
• linked clusters that resemble constellations;
• dense fields that resemble nebulae but resolve into filaments and micro-networks;
• wave, vortex, or cloud structures that continue the central configuration;
• a small number of apparently isolated points when they clearly read as arising or fading network fragments.

Not permitted:
• literal planets, galaxies, comets, or astronomical scenery without doctrinal necessity;
• a random star layer placed behind an otherwise complete image;
• homogeneous fog or nebula texture not constructed from the same network;
• a cosmic landscape containing a separate Buddhist object;
• decorative light dust with no role in conditions, relations, arising, or cessation.

Operational test:
If the relational structure were removed, would an ordinary star field remain? If yes, it is decorative cosmos. If no, because every star-like element is a node or fragment of the shared field, it is canonical Nameless Dhamma morphology.

4.2 White background
The inverted mode uses black and dark-gray networks. Forms emerge through line density rather than luminous glow. White space remains materially empty. The same ontological and recursive rules continue to apply.

4.3 Gray background
Gray may be used for analytical, intermediate, or less dramatic works. It must not turn the image into conventional digital illustration.

4.4 Light temperature
Neutral white:
clarity, paññā, samādhi, direct seeing, equanimity, Dhamma, liberation.

Almost imperceptibly warm ivory-white:
pleasantness, attraction, adhesion, appropriation, the seductive side of saṃsāra.

Almost imperceptibly cool white:
aversion, pain, harshness, corpse-like qualities, fear, the repellent side of saṃsāra.

Warm and cool light must remain fundamentally white. No obvious gold, amber, yellow, cyan, or blue coloration.

4.5 Reference palette

These values are sRGB D65 design and post-production targets. They control relationships between tones; they do not authorize visible colorfulness. Exact pixel matching is required only in controlled manual or post-production output. Generative output is assessed by representative sampling and perceptual equivalence against the current Palette Standards sheet in the Visual Production Registry.

• Absolute black background: #000000
• Pure white background or peak neutral highlight: #FFFFFF
• Neutral network base: #F2F2F0
• Warm ivory-white network base: #F3F0E8
• Warm peak highlight: #FFFDF8
• Cool-white network base: #EDF1F3
• Cool peak highlight: #FAFCFD
• Dark network on white: #111111
• Secondary neutral gray: #727270
• Faint divider or remote filament on black: #3A3A38
• Faint divider or remote filament on white: #D0D0CC

Warm and cool variants must remain close enough to neutral white that the difference is experienced as a bias, not read as yellow, gold, cyan, or blue.

Palette compliance protocol:
• Color space: sRGB D65; comparisons use CIELAB and CIEDE2000 (ΔE00).
• Sample three to five representative midtone and bright network regions. Exclude clipped highlights, transparent halos, background, antialiased edges, and deliberately dark filaments.
• Controlled manual or post-production output: median ΔE00 ≤ 3 and no representative sample > 5.
• Generative output: median ΔE00 ≤ 6 and no representative sample > 10.
• Neutral white: C*ab ≤ 3 and b* between -1.5 and +2.5.
• Warm ivory-white: C*ab ≤ 8 and b* between +2.0 and +7.0.
• Cool white: C*ab ≤ 6 and b* between -4.0 and -0.5.
• Black empty regions: at least 95% of sampled pixels have R, G, and B ≤ 5.
• White empty regions: at least 95% of sampled pixels have R, G, and B ≥ 248.
• Numerical compliance never overrides the visual gate: the work must read as fundamentally monochrome at first glance, and warm or cool bias should become clear mainly in side-by-side comparison with palette swatches and an Active corpus reference on the same calibrated display.

The complete role-by-role values and thresholds are maintained in the Visual Production Registry. Palette codes in this section are design targets; their audit status remains provisional until confirmed against a sufficient set of approved works.

5. Field-density modes

The project uses one visual language with three density modes, not separate stylistic dialects.

5.1 Sparse field
• extensive negative space;
• few nodes and connections;
• one dominant anchor;
• suited to emblems, nirodha, Nibbāna, release, and highly condensed Dhamma statements.

5.2 Connected field
• clearly visible relations and nodes;
• moderate density;
• default mode for most iconic and scenic works;
• strong balance between readability and structural depth.

5.3 Condensed field
• dense micro-networks and clustered systems;
• constellation-like and cloud-like concentrations;
• suited to saṃsāra, aggregates, dependent arising, cognitive construction, and moha;
• must still preserve hierarchy and negative space.

6. Minimalism and hierarchy

Minimalism means few independent semantic units, not necessarily few lines.

Normative structure:
• one primary thesis;
• one semantic anchor;
• usually two to five supporting motifs;
• one dominant path of attention;
• one zero zone;
• deliberate negative space.

High microstructural complexity is permitted. Semantic clutter is not.

7. Anti-canon

Do not use:
• meaningless esotericism;
• arbitrary sacred geometry;
• symbols without a precise doctrinal function;
• decorative cosmic scenery;
• fantasy ornament;
• saturated color;
• golden religious glow;
• heavy opaque masses;
• independent hard-edged objects;
• skulls, chains, demons, or monsters used only for atmosphere;
• chaotic particle explosions;
• uniform detail across the whole surface;
• text used to compensate for weak symbolism;
• a foreign visual style with a network merely overlaid on top.

II. COMPOSITION CLASSIFIER

Choose one primary family for the work as a whole. A simple work may use no more than one secondary modifier. A composite work—such as a triptych, multi-panel sequence, segmented wheel, or nested system—may assign a different family and a different field-density mode to an individual panel or segment when required by the thesis. The project specification must declare the whole-work family, whole-work modifier, whole-work function, whole-work density mode or density trajectory, and every panel- or segment-level family, density mode, local thesis, and local function. Local architectures remain subordinate to the overall reading and may not compete for the first level of attention. Density variation must form an intentional causal trajectory rather than arbitrary stylistic fluctuation; for example, Condensed → Connected → Sparse may express nibbidā → virāga → nirodha.

1. Iconic
One dominant recognizable image.
Use for one quality, one Dhamma, one principle, or one fused symbol.
Examples: Dhamma wheel, Buddha, empty clothing, stone face, lotus-skull fusion.

2. Scenic
Meaning is carried by action, contact, encounter, guarding, holding, release, or passage.
Examples: guardian at a threshold, figure among defilements, hand gripping a network, movement through a dissolving system.
Possible modifier: threshold scene.

3. Sequential
Meaning depends on change through time.
Use for stages, insight progression, arising and cessation, causal sequence, nibbida → virāga → nirodha, or formation and release of appropriation.

4. Radial
The structure organizes around a center or repeats cyclically.
Use for saṃsāra, cycles, aggregates, factors, and systems with comparable components.

5. Networked
Relations and conditionality are the main content.
Use for paṭiccasamuppāda, cognitive construction, distributed processes, appropriation networks, and formation of the experienced world.

6. Polar
Meaning depends on contrast between two modes.
Use for grasping and release, moha and clarity, pleasant and unpleasant, self and impersonality, conditioned and ceased, living tissue and skeleton, or inverse light fields.

Decision tree:
1. Is the thesis primarily a contrast between two modes? → Polar.
2. Does the thesis depend on stages through time? → Sequential.
3. Is repetition or cyclic return primary? → Radial.
4. Are multiple conditions and relations primary? → Networked.
5. Is action, contact, encounter, or threshold primary? → Scenic.
6. Can the thesis be condensed into one symbol or state? → Iconic.

Composition passport:
• whole-work primary family;
• whole-work secondary modifier;
• panel or segment families, if composite;
• whole-work function;
• panel or segment local theses and functions, if composite;
• temporal mode;
• axis;
• focus type;
• symmetry;
• spatial mode;
• whole-work density mode or density trajectory;
• panel or segment density modes, if composite;
• light polarity;
• scale;
• movement;
• aspect ratio.

MACHINE CANON — VERSION 1.5.1

Nameless Dhamma visual language. There are no independent objects anywhere in the image. Background, figure, anatomy, symbols, architecture, light, and space are temporary configurations of one continuous conditional field. Construct every visible form from the same recursive network of fine filaments, nodes, intersections, and translucent connections. At macro, meso, and micro scale, the structure must continue to imply further networks rather than solid material. Forms are recognizable but non-substantial: their boundaries arise, stabilize briefly, loosen, and dissolve into the surrounding field. Show arising, alteration, and cessation through changing conditions, never through decorative explosions. Use one precise Dhamma thesis, one dominant semantic anchor, restrained supporting motifs, clear hierarchy, and deliberate negative space. When a mature DAE result is supplied, preserve its analytical target, constitutive organization, temporal profile, decomposition ceiling, uncertainty, and decisive discriminators in the recognizable configuration; do not convert constitution into a PAE-owned causal relation or force PAE when conditioned relations do not materially change the image. When a verified PLM practice reduction is supplied, prioritize its central hinge as the thesis and dominant attention path; use a broader PAE conditioned network as supporting depth only when it changes what the viewer must understand. Do not force maximum causal enumeration merely because a full map exists. The background must reveal conditions, relations, or consequences; it is never independent scenery. Include a background-colored absence where a stable core would be expected. No eternal observer, soul, independent witness, or metaphysical center. Match network handwriting, node morphology, filament behavior, edge dissolution, and recurring constructions to the selected canonical corpus references. Do not invent a new rendering grammar unless it is explicitly declared as a Candidate visual unit. Reference transfer must be real: when literal visual inheritance is required, every selected Active ND-REF or ND-UNIT source must be attached through visual conditioning or image-editing input. A textual ID, filename, link, or verbal description alone is not a visual reference. If the chosen generator cannot accept visual input, switch to an image-capable tool or use an explicit controlled editor or manual-composite route before claiming reference fidelity.

For black-background works, black represents absence rather than literal outer space. Star-like nodes, constellation-like clusters, and nebula-like concentrations are permitted only when they are visible structures of the same conditional network and perform a causal or semantic function. Do not add literal planets, galaxies, comets, random star fields, homogeneous fog, or decorative cosmic dust. Use one whole-work density mode—sparse, connected, or condensed—unless a declared composite work requires panel- or segment-level density modes. In such works, preserve one coherent causal density trajectory and state every local mode explicitly. Use fundamentally monochrome light: neutral white for clarity, paññā, samādhi, Dhamma, equanimity, and liberation; almost imperceptibly warm ivory-white for attractive, adhesive, and pleasurable saṃsāric processes; almost imperceptibly cool white for painful, aversive, corpse-like, or harsh processes. Warm and cool light must remain essentially white. Default palette targets: absolute black #000000; pure white #FFFFFF; neutral network #F2F2F0; warm ivory network #F3F0E8; cool network #EDF1F3; dark network on white #111111. Apply the current sRGB D65, CIELAB, ΔE00, chroma, pixel-gate, and visual-gate standards from the Visual Production Registry. For white-background works, construct all forms from black and dark-gray networks.

No meaningless esotericism, arbitrary sacred geometry, fantasy ornament, saturated color, golden religious glow, heavy opaque masses, independent hard-edged objects, arbitrary skulls, chains, demons, uniform detail, chaotic particle explosions, or symbols without precise doctrinal function.

V. SEMANTIC LEXICON

The lexicon offers tested anchors; it is not a catalogue of mandatory symbols.

Upekkhā
Anchors: unmoved face, balanced axis, figure within opposing flows.
Risk: emotional suppression, coldness, or insensibility.

Anattā
Anchors: empty clothing, absent body, configuration without a center.
Risk: nihilism or a hidden observer of emptiness.

Sati
Anchors: guardian, threshold, retained trace, process not lost from recognition.
Risk: eternal witness, control, combat, or omniscient eye.

Nirodha
Anchors: terminating path, unsupported configuration, relations no longer feeding form.
Risk: destruction of a being or dramatic annihilation.

Saṃsāra
Anchors: wheel, repeated sequence, returning figures, closing pathways.
Risk: decorative mandala or mystical cosmology without causal structure.

Vedanā
Anchors: warm attractive concentration, cool repellent concentration, neutral current.
Risk: reducing vedanā to emotion.

Paññā
Anchors: neutral clarity, revealing cut, structure becoming transparent.
Risk: mystical light treated as an independent force.

Upādāna
Anchors: self-closing relations, network sustaining its own configuration, filament-hands tightening the field.
Risk: literal chains without appropriation.

Moha
Anchors: misconnected network, obscured relations, configuration mistaking itself for an object.
Risk: merely darkness, evil, or demonization.

Nibbida
Anchors: opening hands, loss of warmth in previously attractive nodes, ending the feeding of an image.
Risk: aversion, hatred, or depression.

Virāga
Anchors: fading attraction, loosening tension, disappearance of warm adhesive light.
Risk: suppression of pleasure or hostile asceticism.

VI. CANON GOVERNANCE AND REFERENCE CORPUS

1. Canon change lifecycle

A new rule passes through three states.

Candidate
A recurring visual solution is discovered in an approved work, a useful correction, or an experimental generation. It may be tested and documented, but it does not alter the canon and may not be silently repeated as a standard.

Tested
The solution has succeeded in at least two independently accepted works, or it is necessary for one foundational brand sign that Savva explicitly approves as a singular exception. Its scope, visual construction, doctrinal purpose, and common failure mode must be documented.

Canonical
A rule becomes canonical only after explicit approval by Savva. It must:
• remain compatible with the one-field ontology;
• improve doctrinal or visual precision;
• have a defined scope;
• include its common misuse;
• be recorded in the Canon Change Log sheet of the Visual Production Registry and summarized in the versioned GitHub architecture/history layer for the non-authoritative Obsidian projection;
• be incorporated into Section I;
• trigger a new document version and Machine Canon recompilation;
• have one unique version entry in the Registry change log and one matching summary at the end of this document.

The assistant may identify Candidates, conduct tests, and recommend canon changes. It may not canonize a rule. Successful images are evidence, not automatic legislation.

3. Visual-unit bank

The reference corpus functions as a gallery of complete works. Repeatable constructions are indexed separately in the Visual Units sheet and, where available, stored as crops in the Visual Units subfolder. A source work may be Active while an extracted unit remains Candidate or Tested. A crop is evidence, not an automatic canonical rule. Only Savva may assign Canonical status to a unit. Registered units may isolate, for example:
• node shape and halo behavior;
• foreground and background filament thickness;
• construction of hands, faces, skeletons, organs, clothing, gates, wheels, and lotus forms from the network;
• edge dissolution;
• zero-zone treatment;
• star-like cluster construction;
• sparse, connected, and condensed field behavior;
• transition between neutral, warm, and cool light.

A new project may instruct: “construct the hand as in [reference file], use node morphology from [reference file], and inherit edge dissolution from [reference crop].” The prompt must identify the function being inherited, not merely cite an entire image vaguely.

Recommended naming for new reference files:
ND-REF-###_[work-or-unit]_[whole-or-crop]_[version].png

Generic legacy file names remain valid only through their stable ND-REF entry in the Corpus Manifest. The Registry is the source of truth for descriptive title, status, scope, supersession, reusable units, and prohibited inheritance.

4. Canon versioning

Every canonical change increments the document version. Minor clarifications and new visual units use 1.x increments. Technical corrections that do not change canonical meaning use a third-component patch increment, such as 1.3.1, and must still update the document header, Machine Canon label, Visual Production Registry, versioned GitHub architecture/history record for the non-authoritative Obsidian projection, and change log. A change to the one-field ontology, core semantic architecture, or primary operational pipeline requires a major version. The Machine Canon must always carry the same version as the document.

VII. AUDIT

1. Audit roles and authority

The audit has two stages.

Assistant pre-audit:
• applies every checklist below;
• compares the work with selected canonical references and relevant neighboring works;
• identifies failures, uncertainties, and unapproved innovations;
• recommends ACCEPT, REGENERATE, or SURGICAL CORRECTION.

Savva’s final audit:
• makes the final decision;
• may accept, reject, request regeneration, or request a surgical correction;
• alone may certify a work as canonical;
• alone may authorize its addition to the canonical reference corpus.

An assistant recommendation is a filter, not an approval.

2. Meaning
• Is the primary thesis understandable without a long explanation?
• Is the semantic anchor recognizable?
• Does every supporting motif have a precise causal or doctrinal function?
• Does the image avoid implying the opposite doctrine?

3. Composition
• Is one whole-work family dominant?
• If the work is composite, are panel- or segment-level families explicitly declared and subordinate to the whole?
• Does any modifier remain subordinate?
• Are the first, second, and third points of attention clear?
• Is negative space preserved?
• Does the work remain readable as a thumbnail?

4. Canon
• Are all forms made from one material field?
• Does the anchor continue into the background?
• Are there independent hard-edged objects?
• Is a zero zone present and semantically justified?
• Does light temperature match the thesis and the reference palette?
• If star-like morphology appears, is it structurally and causally integrated?
• Is there decorative cosmos, arbitrary esotericism, or purposeless symbolism?
• Does the network handwriting match the selected canonical references unless a new Candidate unit is explicitly declared?

5. Process
• Is arising, alteration, or cessation visible?
• Is dissolution tied to weakening conditions?
• Does dissolution avoid explosion aesthetics?
• Is emptiness prevented from becoming a separate metaphysical entity?
• Is there a hidden eternal observer?

6. Corpus and series comparison
• Has the work been compared with the canonical reference corpus?
• Does it unintentionally repeat a semantic anchor already assigned to another card or work?
• If an anchor is repeated, is the repetition necessary, deliberate, and documented?
• Are node shape, filament behavior, line hierarchy, edge dissolution, and recurring anatomical constructions consistent with the selected references?
• Is field density, anchor scale, contrast, and negative-space ratio coherent with neighboring works in the same composition family or series?
• Does the work create an unapproved new visual grammar instead of using the established visual-unit bank?
• If a new unit is valuable, has it been marked Candidate rather than silently treated as canonical?
• Does the image add a distinct doctrinal function to the series rather than merely restaging an existing card?

7. Severity classification

FATAL — doctrinal distortion; false subject; independent self-existing object; incorrect exact Thai, Pāli, or other required text; failure of the primary thesis; failure of the whole-work architecture; or an unverified claim of canonical compliance.

MAJOR — wrong whole-work or panel composition family; unjustified density mode or density trajectory; occupied-anchor duplication without purpose; decorative background; failed attention hierarchy; material conflict with Active references; or a substantial corpus/series inconsistency.

MINOR — local anatomy or proportion; slight temperature drift within the correct role; one excess node; small line-weight inconsistency; or one localized departure from a selected unit that does not alter thesis, architecture, or ontology.

Decision thresholds:
• any Fatal failure → REGENERATE;
• two or more Major failures → REGENERATE;
• one localized Major failure, or several Minor failures with a stable foundation → SURGICAL CORRECTION;
• only negligible Minor failures → assistant may recommend ACCEPT, still subject to Savva’s final audit.

8. Decision

ACCEPT — no Fatal failure exists; no unresolved Major failure remains; thesis, architecture, ontology, palette role, and corpus coherence are correct. Negligible Minor imperfections do not alter meaning. This recommendation still requires Savva’s final decision.

REGENERATE — any Fatal failure exists, or multiple Major failures show that thesis, hierarchy, whole-work architecture, segment architecture, density trajectory, one-field ontology, exact text, or corpus coherence is wrong. Do not repair structural failures through accumulated local edits.

SURGICAL CORRECTION — the foundation is correct and the remaining problem is one localized Major failure or a bounded set of Minor failures, such as anatomy, proportion, symbol placement, light temperature, one excess element, one incorrect relation, or one local departure from a selected visual unit. Every surgical-edit prompt must explicitly preserve all unaffected aspects.

VIII. OPERATIONAL PROTOCOL

Before every Nameless Dhamma image task:

1. Open this document.
2. Open the Visual Production Registry: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/13TgCxf91Rb0fJd3APqb_b83U-xvCKuCJeI98LCxXPg4/edit. Confirm the current Canon version, Active corpus statuses, relevant Visual Units, Palette Standards, Canon Change Log, and the relevant 108-Card Atlas row.
3. Open and inspect only the Active references selected from the Corpus Manifest in the canonical folder: https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1DUqJnVU8KseRh05R6r1UormDz3EBLIUX.
4. Retrieve the approved Dhamma basis from Nameless Dhamma Control, Approved Knowledge, a verified source, or an explicitly labeled synthesis.
4a. Classify the upstream representation as DIRECT, CONSTITUTIVE_ANALYSIS, PRACTICE_PRIORITY, FULL_CAUSAL_MAP, or HYBRID. When a mature DAE result materially determines what the represented process is or how it is constituted, preserve its target, profile, uncertainty, and decisive discriminators; do not invent a causal relation. When a mature PLM pointer suits the purpose, use it to set the thesis and attention hierarchy; consult the PAE map only for supporting relations that materially change the image.
5. Formulate one primary Dhamma thesis and, for a composite work, each panel or segment local thesis and function.
6. Classify the work at two possible levels: declare one whole-work composition family and function, then declare necessary panel- or segment-level families and functions for a composite work.
7. Declare the whole-work density mode or density trajectory. For a composite work, declare each panel or segment density mode and its causal relation to the sequence.
8. Select relevant Active ND-REF entries and registered ND-UNIT entries. Record IDs, links, statuses, source regions, inherited functions, literal constraints, and allowed variation.
9. Complete the project specification, including Registry version, 108-Card Atlas row, palette target and tolerance, whole-work and local density modes, references, units, and any new Candidate unit.
10. Choose the technical tool route for generation, editing, and typography.
10a. Verify whether the chosen generator accepts each selected Active reference as actual visual input through visual conditioning or image editing, rather than only as a textual ID, filename, link, or description.
10b. Attach the selected reference files as actual visual inputs and record the transfer mode and inherited function of each file.
10c. If the primary generator is text-only or cannot accept the required reference input, switch to an image-capable generator or use an explicit controlled editor or manual-composite route before claiming reference fidelity.
11. Check for false subject, arbitrary symbolism, decorative cosmos, duplicate reserved anchors, and unapproved changes to the established network handwriting.
12. Compile the final prompt using Machine Canon version 1.5.1, the current Registry, and selected Active corpus references.
13. Generate one primary version.
14. The assistant performs the preliminary audit, assigns Fatal/Major/Minor severity, compares the work with Active references and neighbouring Atlas rows, evaluates palette thresholds, and recommends ACCEPT, REGENERATE, or SURGICAL CORRECTION.
15. Savva alone makes the final decision. The assistant’s recommendation never constitutes final acceptance.
16. A work becomes an Active canonical reference only after Savva explicitly certifies it, the file is placed in the corpus folder, and its Corpus Manifest status is set to Active. Savva may perform these actions directly, or the assistant may do so only after an explicit request or permission for that specific work.
17. Save the project specification, final prompt, selected ND-REF and ND-UNIT IDs, image, severity audit, Savva’s decision, technical route, Atlas status, and any Candidate findings in the project system.

Drive-unavailable rule:
If the current Canon, Visual Production Registry, required Active references, or relevant Atlas row cannot be opened, no output may be described as canonical, approved, or corpus-compliant. Only Draft or Experimental work may proceed, explicitly labelled “Canonical references not verified.” Final canonical generation must wait until the sources can be verified.

This document has priority over fragmented earlier instructions. The Visual Production Registry is the operational source of truth for reference status, visual-unit status, palette standards, series allocation, and version history. A canonical reference requires both corpus-folder presence and Active Manifest status. Images elsewhere in Drive, ChatGPT Library, the historical external provider archive, or conversation history remain non-canonical unless Savva explicitly certifies them and the two-part admission rule is completed.
